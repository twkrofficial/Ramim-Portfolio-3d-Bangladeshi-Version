# Bangladeshi Visual-Design Research — Sylhet-Rooted 3D Portfolio World

## 1. Sylhet Region Reference Notes

- **Tea gardens (Sreemangal / Malnichhara):** Low rolling hills carpeted with waist-high tea bushes in "rolling green carpets" on terraced slopes — 150+ estates. The visual signature is the *stripe rhythm*: parallel plucking rows of dense yellow-green bush tops separated by darker shadow furrows, punctuated by tall skinny shade trees (silver oak) with bare trunks and small canopies. Perfect for a game: rows read as repeating hex-green bands even at low poly count. ([Royal Bengal Tours](https://royalbengaltours.com/destinations/sylhet-and-sreemangal-rainforests-and-tea/), [Asian Geographical](https://asiangeographical.com/bangladesh/sylhet))
- **Haor wetlands (Hakaluki, Tanguar):** Vast seasonal inland seas — in monsoon they are silver-grey mirror water to the horizon with island villages on raised earthen mounds; in dry season, emerald grassland with scattered pools. Sky and water share one tone.
- **Ratargul swamp forest:** Bangladesh's only freshwater swamp forest, the "Amazon of Bangladesh" — Hijal and Koroch trees standing 20–30 ft deep in tannin-tinted green-brown water; canoes glide through mirror channels; light comes as dappled shafts through dense canopy. Game translation: dark teal-green water plane + tree trunks emerging directly from water + strong reflection. ([Sylhet Tourist Guide](https://sylhettouristguide.com/place/ratargul), [Discover with Ruhul](https://discoverwithruhul.com/2025/04/24/%F0%9F%8C%BF-ratargul-swamp-forest-the-submerged-forest-of-sylhet/))
- **Surma river:** Wide, slow, silty grey-brown-green; low banks; country boats and bamboo fish traps.
- **Jaflong / Lalakhal:** The one place in Bangladesh with *turquoise* water (Dawki river, colored pebbles) — a good "special zone" accent color that is still authentic. ([Homejourney](https://www.homejourney.sg/blog/sylhet-tea-gardens-complete-travel-guide-homejourney))

## 2. Rural Visual Staples (asset checklist)

| Asset | Authentic detail |
|---|---|
| Tin-roof house | Corrugated iron on bamboo/timber frame; walls of tin, mud, or woven bamboo (*bera*); roofs: raw silver, rust-streaked, or painted deep green / oxide red. Pitched, often with a small veranda. |
| Bamboo grove | Clumping, arching stands at village edges; pale yellow-green culms. |
| Banana plants | Clustered by every homestead; huge torn paddle leaves, slightly blue-green. |
| Rice paddy | Flooded emerald squares with low mud bunds (aisles walkable — good for paths); golden at harvest. |
| Jute | Tall (2–3 m) slender stalks, vivid light green; retting ponds with grey bundles. |
| Dirt roads | Raised earthen embankment roads, red-brown laterite/clay tone, often lined with palms. |
| Pond + shapla | Every homestead has a *pukur*; white/pink water lily (shapla) is the national flower — white petals, yellow center, round pads. |
| Nouka (country boat) | Curved crescent hull, dark tarred wood, often a woven bamboo canopy (*chhoi*); dinghi and larger sampan-style hulls. |
| Krishnachura | Delonix regia, May–June bloom, flame red-orange canopy — your existing red "birch" trees can honestly stand in for these; just widen the canopy silhouette if possible. |
| Banyan (bot gach) | Village-square giant with aerial roots; the traditional meeting/shade spot — natural "hub" landmark. |

## 3. Urban Flavor

- **Rickshaws:** UNESCO-listed (2023) rolling folk art — "very bold, bright, raw colors like fluorescent green and dark red"; yellow, red, blue, green dominate; painted tin back-panels with movie stars, peacocks, Taj Mahal, floral vines; tinsel and plastic fringe. ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/rickshaws-and-rickshaw-painting-dhaka-inscribed-unesco-representative-list-intangible-cultural), [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/life-living/news/the-story-rickshaw-art-bangladesh-3488756))
- **Shop signage:** Dense hand-painted Bangla lettering, red/yellow/blue on white or yellow boards, stacked vertically on buildings.
- **Brick:** With no natural stone in the delta, fired red brick is *the* construction material — 7,000+ kilns, tall slim chimneys over orange-red brick stacks in dry-season fields. A brick-kiln chimney is an instantly Bangladeshi silhouette. ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/328956/what-bangladesh-is-doing-to-shift-to-%E2%80%98green), [Al Jazeera photo essay](https://www.aljazeera.com/indepth/inpictures/2014/05/pictures-brick-fields-banglade-2014517134431553324.html))
- **Star Mosque (Tara Masjid), Old Dhaka:** White marble domes covered in hundreds of blue stars; *Chinitikri* mosaic of broken China porcelain — white ground, cobalt/turquoise stars, floral tiles. Great source for a decorative tile texture. ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/star-mosque), [Nijhoom](https://nijhoom.com/star-mosque/))
- **Terracotta:** Kantajew/Paharpur-style relief panels — repeatable red-brown square tiles with figurative carving; use as trim on brick buildings.

## 4. Cultural Color Systems

- **Flag:** bottle green field `#006A4E`, red disc `#F42A41` (conventional hexes; no official digital spec exists — dye-based spec only). ([flagcolorcodes.com](https://www.flagcolorcodes.com/bangladesh), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Bangladesh), [Tauheed on the missing official spec](https://mtauheed.com/there-are-no-official-digital-specs-for-the-colours-of-bangladesh-national-flag/))
- **Pohela Boishakh:** anchor is red + white (lal-paar white saree with red border), expanded with yellow/marigold, and folk earth tones — "clay red, leafy green, sky blue," terracotta, mustard. ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/features/mode/pahela-baishakh-bright-colours-heritage-motifs-and-contemporary-flair-1113381), [Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/lifestyle/culture/what-to-try-instead-of-traditional-red-and-white-in-this-years-pohela-boishakh))
- **Nakshi kantha:** running-stitch embroidery on layered white/cream cotton; motif colors are red, indigo blue, yellow, green, black — lotus, tree-of-life, paisley, spiral (*shatadala*) motifs. Ideal for UI borders and loading screens.
- **Alpana:** white rice-paste motifs (lotus, vines, fish, footprints of Lakshmi) painted on red-oxide or earthen floors — a ready-made decal system: white patterns on `#8E3B2F` ground.

### Sky Gradients (3-stop, top → horizon)
| Time | Stops | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Day (humid golden haze) | `#7FB2D9` → `#C9D9C9` → `#F2D9A0` | Bengal daylight is never crisp blue — humidity washes the zenith and the horizon glows warm dust-gold. |
| Monsoon | `#5A6B7A` → `#8494A0` → `#AEB8B5` | Flat blue-grey nimbus deck fading to pale wet horizon; pair with desaturated terrain +10% darker. |
| Night (tropical) | `#101B33` → `#233252` → `#4A3B52` | Deep warm-navy zenith to violet horizon holding leftover heat-haze; never pitch black in a humid sky. |

### Design guidance for cohesion
1. Keep terrain greens warm/yellow-shifted and reserve the cool blue-greens for banana leaves, tea shadows, and Ratargul — this warm/cool split is what reads "Bengal" vs generic fantasy.
2. Let `#006A4E` and `#F42A41` appear *sparingly* in the environment (a roof, a flag, a rickshaw hood) so the flag pairing feels earned.
3. The alpana system (white `#F5F1E6` motifs on `#8E3B2F`) works as portals/waypoint decals; nakshi kantha stitch patterns work as UI frame texture.
4. Krishnachura retint of the red birch: shift foliage toward `#E64A19` (orange-red, not crimson) and it becomes authentically flame-tree.
